### Field, lines and landmarks (22)

| Term | Meaning | Also called |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Field of play** | The area inside the sidelines and between the two goal lines. The sidelines and end lines themselves are out of bounds. | the field |
| **Sideline** | The line along each long side of the field. Touching it means out of bounds. | boundary line |
| **Goal line** | The line at the front of each end zone, 100 yards apart. It belongs to the end zone, so the ball reaching it in a runner's possession is a touchdown. | the goal |
| **End zone** | The 10-yard area between the goal line and the end line at each end of the field. |  |
| **End line** | The line at the back of each end zone. Touching it means out of bounds. | back line |
| **Yard line** | A line across the field every 5 yards, counted from each goal line up to the 50. | five-yard line |
| **Yard-line numbers** | The numbers painted at every 10-yard line, standing on their bottoms toward the nearer sideline. Optional on high school fields; when used they are about 6 feet tall with their tops 9 yards from the sideline. | the numbers |
| **Hash marks** | Short lines at every yard, 53 feet 4 inches (17.8 yards) in from each sideline, so they split the field into thirds. Every play starts with the ball on or between them. High school hashes are farther apart than college or NFL hashes, so a hash puts more room on the field side. | hashes, inbounds lines |
| **Ball spot** | Where the ball sits across the field before the snap, seen from the offense - on the left hash, on the right hash, or in the middle. | hash, ball placement |
| **Field side** | With the ball on a hash, the side with more room to the far sideline (about 35.6 yards). | wide side, field |
| **Boundary side** | With the ball on a hash, the side with less room to the near sideline (about 17.8 yards). | short side, boundary |
| **Ball in the middle** | The ball spotted between the hashes, about 26.7 yards from each sideline, so there is no field or boundary side. | middle of the field (ball spot) |
| **Line of scrimmage** | The yard line where a play starts. Each team has its own line at its end of the ball, so the two lines are one ball-length apart. | LOS, scrimmage line, the line |
| **Neutral zone** | The space between the two lines of scrimmage, as long as the ball. Before the snap only the snapper may be in it. | NZ |
| **Expanded neutral zone** | After the snap on a pass play, the neutral zone stretches up to 2 yards past the defense's line. Offensive linemen may block in it but may not go past it before a pass that crosses the line is thrown. | ENZ |
| **Line to gain** | The yard line the offense must reach for a first down - 10 yards from where the series started, or the goal line if that is closer. | first-down line, the sticks, the chains |
| **End man on the line** | The last offensive player on the line of scrimmage on one side - a tackle, or a tight end attached to him. | EMOL, EMLOS |
| **Tackle box** | Coaching word for the area between the two offensive tackles, from the line of scrimmage back. High school rules use the free-blocking zone instead. |  |
| **Free-blocking zone** | A rectangle set at the snap, 4 yards to each side of the ball and 3 yards behind each team's line. It ends once the ball leaves it. A passer outside it may throw the ball away if the throw reaches the line of scrimmage. | FBZ |
| **Box** | The area in front of the offensive line where defenders can stop the run - from 1 yard outside the end man on the line on each side, 6 yards deep. "Count the box" means count the defenders standing in it. | the box, run box |
| **Apex** | The spot halfway between the end man on the line and the next receiver out (#2), at linebacker depth. A defender there can help on the run or cover to that side. | apexed |
| **Receiver numbering** | Counting the receivers on each side of the ball from the sideline in - #1 is the widest, then #2, then #3. Reads and coverages are described with these numbers. | #1, #2, #3, number one receiver, number two receiver, number three receiver |

### NFHS rules words (33)

| Term | Meaning | Also called |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Snap** | The center handing or passing the ball back between his legs to start the play. Before the snap the ball is ready for play; after it, the ball is live. | hike |
| **Down** | One play, from the snap until the ball is dead. The offense has 4 downs to reach the line to gain. | play |
| **First down** | Reaching the line to gain, which gives the offense a new set of 4 downs. | move the chains, moving the sticks |
| **Dead ball** | The ball after the play is over - the runner is down or out of bounds, a pass is incomplete, a team scores, or an official blows the whistle. | whistle, play over |
| **Runner** | The player holding a live ball. The quarterback is a runner from the moment he has the snap until he hands off or throws. | ball carrier |
| **Runner down** | In high school a runner is down as soon as any part of his body other than a hand or foot touches the ground, even if no defender touched him. A quarterback whose knee touches the ground is down there. | knee down |
| **Passer** | The player who throws a legal forward pass. He stays the passer until the pass ends or he moves to take part in the play; the rules protect him in that time. |  |
| **Forward pass** | A throw that first moves toward the opponent's goal line. One legal forward pass per down, thrown from behind the line of scrimmage. | pass |
| **Illegal forward pass** | A forward pass thrown with the passer's whole body past the line of scrimmage, a second forward pass in the same down, or a pass after the ball has changed teams. |  |
| **Backward pass** | A pass or pitch that does not go forward; sideways counts as backward. If it hits the ground it is still a live ball, and either team can recover it. | lateral, pitch |
| **Fumble** | Losing possession of the ball other than by a pass or kick. A fumbled ball is live and either team can recover it. | loose ball |
| **Eligible receiver** | An offensive player who may catch a forward pass - one at either end of the line or in the backfield, wearing a number 1-49 or 80-99. | eligible |
| **Ineligible receiver** | An offensive player who may not catch a forward pass - interior linemen and anyone numbered 50-79 (except in kick formations). | ineligible |
| **Ineligible downfield** | An ineligible player more than 2 yards past the line of scrimmage when a pass that crosses the line is thrown. It matters on screens and run-pass options. | illegal man downfield, linemen downfield |
| **Intentional grounding** | Throwing an incomplete forward pass on purpose to avoid losing yards or to stop the clock. Two exceptions - the spike, and the throwaway from outside the free-blocking zone. | grounding |
| **Spike** | Throwing the ball straight into the ground right after the snap to stop the clock. Legal for the player directly behind the snapper, immediately after a clean snap. | spike the ball, clock play |
| **Throwaway** | Throwing the ball where no one can catch it to avoid a sack. In high school it is legal only if the passer is outside the free-blocking zone and the ball reaches the line of scrimmage (or that line extended out of bounds). | throw it away |
| **Sack** | The quarterback tackled behind the line of scrimmage before he throws. Not a rule-book word, but the game counts it. |  |
| **Safety** | The offense is downed, or commits certain fouls, in its own end zone after putting the ball there. The defense scores 2 points and the offense must kick to them from its own 20. |  |
| **Pass interference** | Illegal contact that keeps a player from catching a legal forward pass that crosses the line. In high school it is 15 yards either way; for the defense it is not an automatic first down. | PI, DPI, OPI |
| **Roughing the passer** | A defender hitting the passer late or illegally after the throw. In high school it is 15 yards and an automatic first down. | roughing |
| **False start** | An offensive movement before the snap that imitates the snap or is meant to pull the defense offside - including a quarterback's sudden head, arm or body jerk while calling the count. A hard count with the voice alone is legal. | flinch |
| **Hard count** | The quarterback stressing a word in the cadence to draw the defense offside. Legal when only the voice changes; moving the head or body to imitate the snap is a false start. |  |
| **Encroachment** | A player in the neutral zone before the snap when the rules forbid it - any offensive player except the snapper once the ball is ready, or a defender who touches an opponent or the ball there. | neutral zone infraction, offside |
| **Legal formation** | At the snap - at least 7 offensive players on the line, at least 5 of them numbered 50-79, and no more than 4 in the backfield. | formation |
| **Motion** | One offensive back moving before the snap. Only one player may be moving at the snap, and not toward the opponent's goal line. | man in motion |
| **Shift** | Two or more offensive players changing position before the snap. After a shift, all 11 must be set for 1 second before the snap. |  |
| **Delay of game** | Not snapping the ball before the play clock runs out. A 5-yard penalty. | delay |
| **Game clock** | The clock for each 12-minute quarter. It stops after incomplete passes, plays out of bounds, scores, penalties, timeouts and first downs. | clock, scoreboard clock |
| **Play clock** | The countdown to snap the ball - 40 seconds from the end of the previous play, or 25 seconds after stoppages such as timeouts and scores. | 40-second clock, 25-second clock |
| **Ready for play** | The referee's signal that the ball is set. After it the offense may snap, and on 25-second plays the play clock starts. | ready signal |
| **Timeout** | A stop in play called by a team (3 per half for each team) or by an official. | TO |
| **Running clock** | The CIF mercy rule - when one team leads by 35 points or more in the 4th quarter, the game clock keeps running except for timeouts, scores and injuries. | mercy rule |

| Term | Meaning | Also called |

### Pocket movement (batch 4) - the pocket, the pass rush and how he moves against it (13)

| Term | Meaning | Also called |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pocket** | The protected area behind the offensive line where the quarterback sets up to throw, roughly tackle to tackle and a few yards deep. | the pocket |
| **Set point** | The spot where the quarterback's drop ends and he sets his feet to throw. | launch point, set spot |
| **Clean pocket** | Every rusher is blocked and the pocket holds: he stays in it and throws on time. | clean |
| **Happy feet** | Moving around or leaving the pocket when it is clean, which throws off the timing of the play. |  |
| **Speed rush** | An edge rusher running around the tackle toward the spot behind the quarterback's set point. | edge rush, edge speed rush |
| **Bull rush** | A rusher driving his blocker straight back toward the quarterback, shrinking the pocket from the inside. | interior push, push |
| **Free rusher** | A rusher no one blocks. The ball has to come out before he gets there (hot route). | unblocked rusher |
| **Blitz pickup** | A blitzer blocked by a back or a lineman, so the quarterback can stay in the pocket and keep reading. | picked up |
| **Pocket collapse** | Pressure breaking through from more than one point, so no spot in the pocket is safe. | collapse, full collapse |
| **Climb** | Stepping up in the pocket toward the line of scrimmage so edge rushers run past behind him. | step up |
| **Pocket slide** | Short shuffle steps sideways in the pocket, base kept and eyes downfield, away from pressure inside. | slide away, shuffle |
| **Escape** | Leaving the pocket outside the tackle box when it collapses, toward the side with room (his throwing-hand side when both are open). | escape the pocket |
| **Off-hand reset** | After moving toward his off-hand side, the quarterback turns his hips and shoulders back before he can throw, which costs time. | reset, flip the hips |
